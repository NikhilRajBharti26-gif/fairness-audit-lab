Audit: four fairness metrics

Each gap is the difference between the two groups, so zero means equal. DP is demographic parity, EOpp is equal opportunity, EOdds is equalized odds, and PP is predictive parity. Teal is under 0.05, brick is over 0.10.

DI is the disparate impact ratio, the lower approval rate divided by the higher. Under 0.8 fails the four-fifths rule.

Three places to intervene

Each fix acts at a different stage. The last one leaves the trained model untouched.

Before training

Preprocessing: reweighing

Each training example gets a weight so that group and outcome look independent to the learner. The feature values stay the same.

During training

In-processing: fairness penalty

The loss gains a term that grows with the squared gap, so the optimizer trades accuracy for parity directly.

After training

Post-processing: group thresholds

One cut-off per group is chosen on training data within the accuracy budget, then applied to new people.

What each metric asks

Demographic parity
Both groups receive positive decisions at the same rate, whatever their true outcomes.
Equal opportunity
Among people who truly qualify, both groups are approved at the same rate.
Equalized odds
Equal approval rates among the qualified and among the unqualified. Stricter than equal opportunity.
Predictive parity
Among people approved, the same share truly qualify, so an approval means the same thing for each group.

Reweighing follows Kamiran and Calmon (2012). Group thresholds follow Hardt, Price and Srebro (2016). The trade-offs follow Kleinberg, Mullainathan and Raghavan (2016) and Chouldechova (2017). Data is synthetic and seeded, so the numbers show how the methods behave, not what a real system would do.
